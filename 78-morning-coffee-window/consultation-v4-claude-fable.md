# Claude Fable 5 medium — V4 geometric critique

Claude inspected the sole person reference, both V3 failures, and the V3 prompt.

## Diagnosis

The reference has a high foremost upper-torso point, a chest-level widening, and an inward return toward the waist. V3-01 and V3-02 instead make the jersey contact mainly at the neckline/shoulders and span as a broad near-planar chord toward the lap. The foremost garment envelope migrates downward, the side silhouette becomes straight or A-line, and shoulder-origin vertical folds dominate. Near-frontal framing plus broad frontal fill further remove outline and form-shadow evidence.

The prompt's concrete tokens—extra-oversized, wide torso, vertical fall, knee-directed gravity folds, air at waist—outweighed its vague/negative chest-preservation statements.

## Recommended positive block

The front of the shirt is not a flat panel. Its foremost point sits high on the torso, at upper-chest level, clearly forward of the abdomen and of the hem. From the collarbone the jersey slopes forward and down and settles in soft, broad contact over the forward convex curve of the upper torso, following that curve in both directions — vertically from collarbone over the crest, and horizontally from one side seam toward the other — as one continuous rounded region, never two separate shapes. Just below the widest forward point the cloth leaves the body at a soft tangent break; from that line downward it hangs slack and vertical, with visible air between fabric and the narrower waist, so the outline steps inward below the chest before the hem drapes over the lap. In the three-quarter view the near side seam bows gently outward at chest level and returns inward toward the waist, keeping the chest level visibly wider than the waist in the silhouette, exactly as in the reference and with no added volume. Drape folds are born at the underside of that forward crest — not at the shoulder seams — and fan downward and slightly outward toward the hem; the fabric above the crest lies smooth and lightly tensioned, the fabric below it is loose. A single soft form shadow under the crest, where the free-hanging cloth turns away from the window light, is what communicates the volume — shading of cloth, never skin detail.

## Supporting edits

- Rotate the torso modestly toward the window so chest-level outward bow and waist return appear in the outline; retain level 70 mm perspective.
- Use off-axis wall bounce so a soft garment form shadow survives.
- Describe the shirt as oversized yet fluid, with enough fabric weight to settle onto the forward curve rather than bridge across it.
- The raised coffee sleeve must remain slack and must not lift/tent the front panel.
- Keep broad contact + tangent-break + free-hang as one unit; trimming any one risks tenting.
- Avoid paired-hemisphere wording; retain one continuous rounded region.
- Avoid hugs/traces language that can create wet-cloth cling.
- Keep the exact-reference/no-added-volume anchor beside silhouette wording.
- Do not raise transparency to solve volume; use opaque-primary garment shading.

## Observed failures only

Flat planar front bridge; foremost shirt point at abdomen/lap; straight or downward-widening side silhouette without waist return; shoulder-origin purely vertical folds; chest rendered narrower/flatter than reference.
