## Diagnosis

The V3 shirt behaves as a flat vertical sheet or diagonal apron. Its surface normals are too uniform, suppressing the reference's natural forward projection, lateral fullness, and chest-to-waist transition. Concrete gravity-curtain language and the negated phrase “straight box-like shirt bridge” likely reinforced the undesired plane. The prompt lacks explicit contact regions, tangent continuity, a release/inflection line, and differentiated cloth behavior above and below that line.

## Recommended positive direction

Describe the jersey as resting in soft broad upper-torso contact, pitching forward from the neckline, wrapping the outer chest width, then leaving the body at a smooth transition into downward gravitational drape with air around the waist. Preserve the authentic profile and side-torso taper beneath fully covering fabric, without cling, skin tracing, or enlargement.

## Supporting edits

- Give the fine-gauge cotton-modal jersey light structural body rather than extremely limp washed drape.
- Use low-contrast backlight plus soft off-axis form shading to communicate the garment surface.
- Remove failure nouns such as “straight box-like shirt bridge” from positive prose.
- Keep observed-failure negatives concise: planar bridge, flat vertical drape, boxy canvas, flattened profile, suspended curtain, apron-like front.

## Risks

Avoid “tenting,” “supported by chest,” paired cup/dome language, “hugging/tracing,” and enlargement-oriented adjectives. These can respectively produce rigid tent geometry, artificial molded cups, intimate cling, or identity distortion.
